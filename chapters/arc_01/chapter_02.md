Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 2: The Baker's Warmth

"A Mender?" Leo repeated, his voice cracking slightly.

The woman, Mina, stepped closer. Up close, she smelled like warm yeast and honey. She had flour behind her ears and on her apron, but her eyes were clear and full of a quiet, gentle energy. She didn't look at Leo like he was a stranger or a threat. She looked at him like he was a long-awaited guest.

"That's right," she said, pointing to the azure pebble in his hand. "Only a Mender can manifest an Azure Heart-Coin on their first try. Most people only get Amber or Rose. You fixed that bird's very soul, didn't you?"

Leo looked down at the coin. It was pulsed with a soft, blue light that felt cool against his palm. He tried to speak, to explain about the sirens, the shop, and the walnut oil, but a sudden wave of exhaustion hit him. His arms felt like they were made of solid lead. The cold heaviness he had felt earlier was spreading to his shoulders.

He swayed on his feet.

"Ah, the Friction Toll," Mina said, her smile turning into a look of concern. She reached out and grabbed his arm. Her touch was warm and steady. "You've been absorbing the bird's stress without knowing how to vent it. Come. My bakery is just across the bridge. You need a cup of Peace-Brew and a long nap."

Leo was too tired to argue. He let her lead him across the meadow. As they walked, the teal grass whispered against his shins, sounding like thousands of tiny, approving voices. 

They reached the edge of the island. Leo stopped, his breath catching in his throat.

Connecting this island to the next was a bridge that looked like it had been grown, not built. It was a high, arching structure made of white, shimmering wood. There were no nails or ropes. The branches of two massive trees had simply reached out and intertwined, forming a sturdy, living path over the endless clouds.

As Leo stepped onto the wood, a low, vibrating hum traveled up through his boots. It felt like a gentle massage for his tired muscles.

"The Singing Bridges," Mina explained, noticing his surprise. "They shake off the dust of the day. Just listen to the rhythm, Leo. Let it carry you."

Across the bridge, Petal-Wharf came into view. It was the most beautiful thing Leo had ever seen. The village was built on a cluster of islands that, from this angle, looked exactly like the white petals of a lily. The houses weren't made of brick or stone. They were large, gnarled trees with trunks that had grown into circular rooms and balconies. Flowers of every color hung from the windows, and small lanterns shaped like gourds provided a soft, amber light.

People were moveing about in a way that felt impossibly slow to Leo. A man was sitting on a porch, knitting with wool that looked like it was made of clouds. Two children were playing a game with glowing pebbles, their laughter sounding like music. No one was shouting. No one was rushing.

Mina led him to a house that smelled like the best bakery on Earth. The door was made of soft bark that yielded slightly when she pushed it open.

"Sit," she commanded, pointing to a chair that looked like it was made of woven silk. 

As Leo sat, the chair seemed to adjust its shape to fit his body. It was so comfortable it almost felt like he was floating. Mina disappeared into a back room and returned a minute later with a ceramic cup. The liquid inside was a deep, translucent blue.

"Peace-Brew," she said, handing it to him. "Drink it all. It will clear the grey lines from your heart."

Leo took a sip. It didn't taste like tea. It tasted like the air on top of a mountain after a fresh rain. It was cool, crisp, and as it hit his stomach, the cold heaviness in his arms began to melt away. For the first time in his life, the constant "Noise" in his head—the deadlines, the bills, the sound of the city—simply stopped.

"Better?" Mina asked, leaning against a wooden counter.

"I can't hear anything," Leo whispered, staring at the cup.

"That's because there's nothing to hear but the world," Mina laughed. "Welcome to the Reach, Leo. Now, tell me. What is your Heart-Story? Where did a Master Mender like you come from?"

Leo looked at her, then at the Azure coin on the table. He took a deep breath, and for the first time since he woke up, he felt like he could finally tell the truth.
