Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 4: The Dust of Ages

The inside of the Mending Spire smelled like time. Not the rot of decay, but the dry, papery smell of old books and forgotten wood.

Barnaby had to duck to get through the main archway. He moved with agonizing slowness, his stone shoulders scraping against the doorframe with a sound like grinding chalk.

"Sorry," the golem hummed, wincing. "I am... wide."

"You're fine," Leo said, stepping into the main hall. "Just don't sneeze."

The room was circular and vast. The walls were made of the same white living wood as the exterior, but here, the surface was smooth and polished. A spiral staircase wound its way up the center of the room, wrapping around a central pillar that pulsed with a faint, dying amber light.

It was beautiful. It was also freezing.

"It's cold," Leo said, his breath misting in the air. "The windows are intact. Why is it so cold?"

"The Hearth is asleep," Barnaby rumbled, pointing a massive finger toward the center of the room.

There, at the base of the central pillar, was a fireplace carved from black stone. It was empty. Not just of wood, but of *warmth*. The air around it felt heavy and stagnant. Leo could see thin Grey Lines clinging to the stone like spiderwebs.

"Entropy," Leo muttered. He walked toward the hearth. "The house is lonely."

As he got closer, he noticed a pile of what looked like brown fuzzy balls huddled in the corner of the fireplace. There were about a dozen of them, shivering.

Leo squinted. "Are those... tennis balls?"

One of the balls uncurled. It had tiny, round ears and a nose that twitched nervously. It looked like a mouse that had eaten too much yeast.

"Muffin-Mice," Barnaby whispered, his voice vibrating the floorboards. "They seek the heat of the Mender's Hearth. But the heat is gone."

Leo knelt. The mice were terrified. They were huddled together for warmth, their fur dull and matted. They smelled faintly of stale cinnamon.

"Hey," Leo said softly. "Rough night?"

The lead mouse squeaked. It sounded like a dog toy being stepped on.

Leo looked at the hearth. The Grey Lines were thick here. The stone was "Frayed" from years of disuse. It needed more than wood; it needed intent.

"I need to wake it up," Leo said. He looked at Barnaby. "Do we have any Sun-Seeds?"

"No," the golem hummed. "But I have... dry leaves."

Barnaby reached into a pouch on his belt and pulled out a handful of crinkled autumn leaves. He offered them to Leo like they were gold.

"Thanks, buddy," Leo said. He placed the leaves in the grate.

He didn't use a match. He used his hand.

He placed his palm against the cold black stone of the mantle. He closed his eyes and searched for the "Hum" of the house. It was faint—a low, raspy wheeze.

*Friction. Cold. Stagnation.*

Leo visualized his sanding block. He imagined smoothing away the cold layer of dust that covered the stone's soul. He pushed his own warmth—the memory of his workshop on Earth, the smell of hot sawdust, the feeling of a job well done—into the rock.

*Wake up.*

The Friction Toll hit him immediately. A wave of ice shot up his arm, making his teeth chatter. He felt like he had just jumped into a frozen lake. But he held on.

*Smooth the edge. Warm the core.*

The stone shuddered. The Grey Lines snapped and dissolved. A spark of golden light jumped from Leo's hand into the dry leaves.

*Whoosh.*

The fire didn't burn orange; it burned a soft, steady gold. The heat wasn't sharp; it was a physical wave of comfort that rolled across the room.

The Muffin-Mice squeaked in unison. They uncurled and rushed toward the fire, forming a ring of fuzzy, vibrating happiness around the flames. Their fur instantly fluffed up, turning a rich, golden brown.

The smell of fresh cinnamon filled the room.

Leo sat back, rubbing his numb arm. "Okay. That worked."

Barnaby sat down next to him, the floor groaning under his weight. He looked at the fire, then at the mice, then at Leo.

"The Spire is singing," Barnaby hummed. "It likes you."

Leo looked at the fire, then at the golem, then at the mice who were now purring like kittens. He touched the silver thimble at his neck.

"It's just a fire, Barnaby," Leo said, though he couldn't stop the small smile forming on his face. "Don't make it weird."
