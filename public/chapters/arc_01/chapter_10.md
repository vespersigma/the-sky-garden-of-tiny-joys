Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 10: The Diagnosis

Leo held the wire with a pair of wooden tongs.

He sat at his kitchen table, staring at the thin, grey strand. It wasn't behaving like metal. It was behaving like a snake. It curled and uncurled slowly, reacting to the heat of the morning sun streaming through the window.

"It shouldn't be here," Leo muttered. "I dreamed it. You can't bring souvenirs back from a dream."

Barnaby was standing outside the window, peering in. He refused to enter the house while the wire was there.

"It smells like... yesterday," Barnaby rumbled. "It smells like the Before-Time."

"It smells like burnt toast and ozone," Leo corrected.

He picked up his sanding block. He needed to test it. If this was just a piece of "Rust," he should be able to smooth it out. He touched the block to the wire.

*Zzzzt.*

A spark of blue electricity jumped from the wire, scorching the wood of the tongs. Leo dropped them.

"Okay," Leo said, blowing on his fingers. "Not rust. Active current. High voltage."

He needed an expert. He needed someone who knew what the world was like before everyone decided to take a nap for five hundred years.

He needed the Librarian.

***

The Village Library was quiet, even by Petal-Wharf standards. It was built inside a hollowed-out **Weeping Willow** that hung over the edge of the island. The floor was made of glass, allowing visitors to look down into the clouds while they read.

Leo found Elder Mist asleep in a hammock made of spider-silk. The old man was snoring softly, a book titled *The Taxonomy of Moss* resting on his chest.

"Elder Mist," Leo whispered.

One of Mist's eyes popped open. It was a startlingly bright violet.

"I was reading that," Mist said, not moving. "I read with my eyelids."

"I need you to look at something," Leo said. He placed the wooden box containing the wire on the glass floor.

Mist sat up. He moved with a fluid, boneless grace that reminded Leo of a ferret. He floated over to the box and peered inside.

His violet eyes widened.

"Where did you get this?" Mist asked, his voice losing its dreamy slur.

"I found it," Leo said. "In a dream."

Mist looked at Leo, then back at the wire. He reached out a long, thin finger. He didn't touch the wire; he touched the air *around* it.

"It has no friction," Mist whispered. "It is pure entropy, but it has been... stabilized."

"The Iron King gave it to me," Leo said. "He said the silence is killing the Sun-Tree."

Mist flinched. He stood up and walked to the window, looking out at the golden tree in the distance. The light was bright and warm, but for the first time, Leo noticed a faint flicker. A tiny pulse of hesitation in the light.

"The history books say the Iron Kings were monsters," Mist said softly. "They say they wanted to drown the world in noise."

"And?" Leo asked.

"And history is written by the people who stayed awake," Mist replied.

He walked to a section of the library that was covered in a heavy velvet curtain. He pulled a cord, and the curtain fell away. Behind it was a shelf of books bound in black leather. They looked heavy. They looked cold.

"These are the **Forbidden Texts**," Mist said. "They were written during the Era of Rust. Master Archi wants them burned, but I... I am a Librarian. I cannot burn words."

He pulled down a book titled *The Thermodynamics of Prayer*. He opened it to a diagram. It showed the Sun-Tree, but it wasn't just a tree. It was a machine. A biological engine that converted "Prayer" (Sound) into Light.

"The theory," Mist said, tapping the page, "was that if the world became too quiet, the tree would starve. It needs noise. It needs... friction."

Leo looked at the diagram. It looked exactly like the engine he had seen in the Iron King's chest.

"So the Iron Kings weren't trying to destroy the tree," Leo realized. "They were trying to jump-start it."

"With a hammer," Mist added. "They were brute-forcing it. And they almost broke reality."

Leo looked at the grey wire in the box. It was still twitching.

"They used a hammer," Leo said, his craftsman's brain spinning. "But maybe... maybe they just needed a screwdriver."

Mist smiled. It was a sad, tired smile.

"Perhaps," the Elder said. "But be careful, Mender. If you try to fix the sun, you might burn your hands."

Leo touched his silver thimble. It was warm against his chest.

"I have gloves," Leo said.
