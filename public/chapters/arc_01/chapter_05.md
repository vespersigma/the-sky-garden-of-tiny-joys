Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 5: The Wind-Rider

Leo woke up to the sound of scratching.

It was a gentle, rhythmic sound, like sandpaper on wood. He opened one eye. The Mending Spire was filled with the soft, silver light of the morning. The fire in the hearth had burned down to glowing embers, but the room was still warm.

He sat up and looked at the foot of his makeshift bed (a pile of empty grain sacks).

There, sitting in a neat row, were three Muffin-Mice. They were staring at him with unblinking black eyes. In front of the middle mouse was a single, perfect acorn. It had been polished until it shone like glass.

"Good morning," Leo croaked, his voice thick with sleep.

The mice squeaked in unison. The middle one pushed the acorn forward with its nose.

"For me?" Leo asked.

The mouse nodded. It was a distinct, human-like nod.

Leo picked up the acorn. It was warm. He could feel a faint "Hum" coming from it—a tiny vibration of gratitude. It wasn't a Heart-Coin, but it felt just as valuable.

"Thank you," Leo said. He placed the acorn in his pocket, next to his silver thimble. "I'll treasure it."

The mice seemed satisfied. They scattered, vanishing into the cracks of the floorboards with a series of soft *thumps*.

Leo stood up and stretched. His body felt stiff, but the "Wet Cement" feeling from the night before was gone. He walked to the window and looked out.

The world was waking up. The Sun-Tree in the distance was beginning to glow gold, sending waves of warm light across the clouds. But something was wrong.

Leo frowned. He pressed his hand against the window pane. The glass was vibrating. Not the smooth, musical hum of the Singing Bridges, but a jagged, irregular rattle. It felt like a cheap car engine idling at a stoplight.

"Do you feel that?"

Leo turned. Barnaby was standing in the doorway. The golem looked agitated. The moss on his shoulders was bristling, and his azure eyes were dim.

"The vibration?" Leo asked.

"The Itch," Barnaby corrected. He scratched at his stone chest. "It sounds like... biting."

Before Leo could answer, a blur of blue and yellow motion shot past the window.

*Whoosh.*

Something fast was coming.

Leo ran to the door and threw it open. A small sky-boat was hurtling toward the Spire. It wasn't gliding on the Gold Current; it was surfing the edge of a fast Blue Current, banking hard to avoid the trees.

The pilot was a young man with wind-blown hair and goggles that covered half his face. He leaned back, pulling hard on the rigging of his silk sail. The boat drifted sideways, skimming the grass, and came to a halt exactly three inches from Barnaby's toes.

"Morning!" the pilot shouted, hopping off the deck. He was wearing a suit made of tight-fitting glowing thread that shimmered in the sun. He looked like he had just drunk five espressos. "Is this the Mender's place? Mina said there was a Mender. I need a Mender."

"I'm Leo," Leo said, leaning against the doorframe. "And you're parking in a flowerbed."

The pilot looked down. "Right. Sorry. I'm Sylas. Current-Surfer. Delivery Boy. General Nuisance. Nice to meet you."

Sylas didn't wait for a handshake. He reached into his boat and pulled out a long, twisted piece of metal. It looked like a pipe, but it was covered in black, oily sludge.

"I found this near the Root Border," Sylas said, his voice dropping. "It was floating in the mist. I tried to bring it to Elder Thorne, but..."

He held the object out.

Leo stepped forward. As soon as he got close to the metal, he felt it.

It wasn't just a vibration. It was a scream.

The air around the pipe was distorted. Grey Lines were snapping off it like sparks. Leo felt a sudden, sharp pain behind his eyes, like he had stared at the sun for too long. It tasted like copper and old blood.

"Get that away from the garden," Leo ordered, his voice hard.

Barnaby took a step back, his stone hands trembling. "It hurts the song."

"I know," Sylas said, looking terrified. "It started humming an hour ago. And since then... the wind has been wrong. The Blue Currents are getting choppy. I almost crashed twice."

Leo looked at the pipe. It was a piece of the **Era of Rust**. It wasn't just debris; it was an antenna.

"It's broadcasting," Leo whispered. He looked at Barnaby, then at the distant horizon where the Root Isles lay hidden in shadow. "Someone turned the machines back on."
