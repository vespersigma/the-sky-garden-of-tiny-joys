Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 6: The Archive of Rust

"Don't touch it."

The voice was high, sharp, and precise. It sounded like a pair of scissors cutting through paper.

Leo, Sylas, and Barnaby stood in the center of the Village Library. It wasn't a cozy room of wood and cushions. It was a hall of glass cases and vacuum-sealed jars. The air smelled of cold ink and peppermint.

Master Archi stood behind a desk of polished white marble. He was wearing white silk gloves and adjusting a monocle that had three different lenses.

"I didn't touch it," Leo said, pointing to the rusted pipe that Sylas had placed on a velvet cloth. "Sylas did."

Archi sighed. It was a short, clipped sound. *Tsk.*

"And now Sylas is contaminated," Archi said, not looking up from his ledger. "He will need to be scrubbed with Glow-Moss before he is allowed near the ancient texts. Sylas, please step three feet to the left. You are breathing on the glass."

Sylas shuffled sideways, looking guilty. "It's just a pipe, Master Archi. But it's... loud."

Archi finally looked up. His eyes were magnified by the monocle, making him look like a very skeptical owl. He walked around the desk. His starched white robes made a distinct *shink-shink* sound with every step.

"It is not just a pipe," Archi corrected. "It is a **Harmonic Disruptor** from the Late Iron Era. Specifically, a piston-valve from a Steam-Beast."

He stopped exactly two feet from the table. He pulled a small, brass watch from his pocket. *Tick. Tick. Tick.* The sound was mechanical and cold, cutting through the organic silence of the room.

"The frequency represents a Class 4 danger," Archi noted, checking the watch. "It is vibrating at a pitch designed to crack **Heart-Stones**. Where did you find this?"

"The Root Border," Sylas said.

Archi’s face paled. He polished his monocle with a silk cloth. One circle. Two circles. Three circles.

"The Root Border," Archi whispered. "That is too close. The containment field must be fraying."

He reached under the desk and pulled out a heavy lead box. "We must seal it. Immediately. If we let it vibrate for another hour, it will shatter every Singing Rose in the village."

Leo stepped forward. "No."

Archi froze. He turned his head slowly, like a turret. "Excuse me, Mender?"

"If you seal it," Leo said, his voice calm but firm, "you just trap the noise inside. It will keep screaming. It will just be a muted scream."

"That is the point," Archi snapped. "Safety. Containment. Order."

"That's not safety," Leo said. "That's denial."

Leo looked at the pipe. The Grey Lines were pulsing violently now. He could feel the "heat" of the entropy radiating off it. It made his teeth ache.

"It's not trying to hurt us," Leo realized. He took a step closer, ignoring Archi’s gasp of protest. "It's in pain. It's vibrating because it's lonely. It was built to be part of a machine, and now it's just... junk."

Leo reached out.

"Do not!" Archi shrieked, his voice losing its precision. "You will absorb the rust! It will turn your heart to lead!"

Leo didn't listen. He didn't use the sanding block. He didn't use the thimble. He used his bare hand.

He grabbed the pipe.

*CRACK.*

The sound was deafening inside Leo's head. It sounded like a train crash. The "Wet Cement" sensation hit him instantly, filling his arm, his chest, his throat. He tasted oil and ash. He fell to his knees, gasping.

"Leo!" Barnaby rumbled, stepping forward. The floor cracked under his sudden movement.

"I'm... fine," Leo wheezed. He gripped the pipe tighter.

He closed his eyes. He visualized the "Noise" of the pipe. It was jagged, sharp, and terrified.

*Shhh,* Leo thought, pushing his own "Earthly Weight" against the rust. *You don't have to scream. You can just be.*

He sanded the edges of the sound. He polished the fear until it was smooth. He took the "Idea" of the piston—a thing made for violence—and reshaped its hum into something else. Something steady. Something useful.

The screaming stopped.

Leo opened his eyes. The black sludge had flaked off, revealing clean, dull iron underneath. The pipe wasn't vibrating anymore. It was humming a low, steady B-Flat.

"It's... singing," Sylas whispered.

Archi stared. He looked from the pipe to Leo, his mouth slightly open. He adjusted his monocle, but his hand was shaking.

"You changed its nature," Archi said softly. "You didn't just mend it. You re-purposed it."

Leo stood up. He felt heavy. Incredibly heavy. He felt like he was wearing a suit of armor. But he smiled.

"It's a flute now," Leo said, tossing the pipe to Sylas. "Or a very heavy wind-chime. Either way, it's not a weapon anymore."

Leo turned to Archi. "You can keep your lead box, Master Archi. We won't be needing it."

Archi looked at the box, then at Leo. For a second, the bureaucrat vanished, and Leo saw a scared man who just wanted the world to be safe.

"Perhaps," Archi said, his voice regaining its clip. "But you have absorbed a dangerous amount of Entropy, Mender. I suggest you visit the Stamen Well before you turn into a statue yourself."
