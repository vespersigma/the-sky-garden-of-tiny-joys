Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 1: The First Sensation

The first thing Leo noticed was the silence.

It wasn't the empty, peaceful silence of a library. It was a heavy, suspicious silence. It felt like the moment before a structural beam snaps. On Earth, silence was a luxury he couldn't afford. His workshop was a symphony of grinding gears, his apartment was a box vibrating with the city's traffic, and his mind was a constant checklist of unpaid invoices and unfinished restorations.

But this? This was unnatural.

Leo opened his eyes, expecting the water-stained drywall of his ceiling. Instead, he was staring at a sky that looked wrong. It was too blue. Not the pale, smog-filtered blue of the city, but a deep, saturated azure that looked like it had been over-corrected in Photoshop. There were no clouds, only these strange, shimmering ribbons of gold light weaving through the atmosphere like optical fibers.

"Great," Leo muttered, his voice sounding flat in the open air. "I'm either dead, or I'm having a very vivid hallucination caused by paint fumes."

He sat up. The ground didn't feel like dirt. It yielded under his weight like high-density foam. He looked down and saw grass—if you could call it that. It was teal, perfectly uniform, and felt more like woven silk than plant matter.

He stood up, instinctively checking his center of gravity. He felt... wrong. Lighter. On Earth, his knees clicked when he stood up too fast. Here, he felt like he was walking on the moon. He took a step, and his foot stayed in the air a fraction of a second longer than physics should allow.

"Low gravity," he noted, tapping his heel against the ground. "Or high oxygen density. Either way, not Earth."

He walked to the edge of the landmass he was on. It ended abruptly. No beach, no cliffside descent. just a sheer drop into an infinite sea of white mist. He leaned over, gripping a tree root that felt warm to the touch. Below him, dozens of other islands drifted in a slow, mathematical spiral. They orbited a massive, glowing tree in the distance that looked less like a plant and more like a fusion reactor made of gold glass.

"Floating islands," Leo said, rubbing his face with calloused hands. "Because of course. Why respect the laws of thermodynamics when you can just float?"

He sighed. He wasn't panicked yet. Panic was inefficient. Panic wasted energy you might need to run away from whatever giant bird inevitably lived on floating islands.

He checked his pockets. Empty. He checked his neck. His fingers brushed against the cold metal of his silver thimble. Good. At least he had his tools. Well, *one* tool.

A sudden, jarring vibration traveled up his arm.

It wasn't a sound. It felt like dragging a piece of silk over a rusty nail. It set his teeth on edge. Leo winced and looked to his left.

A few meters away, a small bird with lavender feathers was thrashing in the grass. Its wing was bent at an unnatural angle. But that wasn't what made Leo sick. It was the air around the bird. It was... fractured. Thin, smoking grey lines were bleeding out of the injury, snapping and hissing like broken live wires.

"Friction," Leo whispered. He didn't know how he knew the word, but it tasted like copper in his mouth.

He approached the bird slowly. "Easy now. I'm not going to eat you. Probably."

The bird let out a static-filled screech. Leo knelt. The grey lines were physical. He could feel the heat radiating from them. They were entropy made visible—the universe unraveling at a single point.

His hand moved on its own. He didn't want to touch it—it looked painful—but his craftsman's brain saw a "Rough Edge" and screamed at him to sand it down.

"Just a little polish," he muttered, closing his eyes.

He touched the wing.

It felt like grabbing a handful of static electricity. The shock jolted through his shoulder, making his teeth rattle. Leo gritted his teeth. He visualized his workshop. He imagined the sanding block in his hand. He didn't try to "Heal" the bird; he just tried to **Smooth the Noise**.

*Rough grain. High grit. Sand it down. Even strokes.*

He pushed his will against the grey lines. They resisted, biting into his skin like acid, but he pushed harder. He felt a heavy, cold sludge moving from the bird into his own arm. It felt like his veins were being filled with wet cement.

"Heavy," he gasped, sweat beading on his forehead. "So heavy."

With a final, mental *snap*, the grey lines vanished.

Leo fell back onto the grass, his chest heaving. His right arm was numb. He felt like he had just carried a sofa up four flights of stairs.

The bird stood up. Its wing was perfect. Not just healed, but aligned with a geometric precision that nature usually didn't bother with. It shook its feathers, sending a puff of golden dust into the air, and looked at Leo with intelligent, black eyes.

It chirped—a pure, resonant sound like a crystal bell—and took off, catching a gold current and vanishing instantly.

"You're welcome," Leo wheezed, staring at the sky.

He tried to sit up, but his arm wouldn't cooperate. It was pinned to the ground. He looked down. Resting on his chest was a small, smooth stone. It glowed with a deep, azure light.

"Payment?" Leo asked dryly. "I usually charge by the hour."

"It's a Heart-Coin," a voice said.

Leo froze. He tilted his head back. Standing over him was a woman who looked like she had been baked rather than born. She smelled of yeast and warm sugar. She was wiping flour off her hands onto an apron, looking at him with a mixture of amusement and concern.

"And you," she said, offering a hand that looked strong enough to knead concrete, "look like you just tried to mend the world before breakfast."
