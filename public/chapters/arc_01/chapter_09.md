Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 9: The Dream of Iron

Sleep in Verdant Reach was not a fading of consciousness. It was a change of venue.

As Leo closed his eyes in the Mending Spire, he felt the familiar pull of gravity vanish. The bed of **Sleep-Grass** beneath him seemed to liquefy, and he sank through the floor, through the roots of the island, and into the silver veins of the world.

He opened his eyes.

He was standing on a bridge made of moonlight. The sky was a swirling nebula of soft, pastel colors—the dreams of his neighbors. To his left, he saw a giant, floating loaf of bread that was singing opera (Mina's dream). To his right, a field of snails were racing at the speed of light (Barnaby's dream).

"The Root-Web," Leo whispered.

In the Shared Dream, communication didn't require words. He could "feel" the location of the village square. It was a bright, warm cluster of lights where the souls of Petal-Wharf were gathering to share stories without speaking.

He took a step toward the light.

*Bzzt.*

A sharp, static noise cut through the silver air. It sounded like a radio tuned between stations. Leo stopped. The bridge beneath his feet rippled.

*Bzzt. Crrrrack.*

The sound wasn't coming from the village. It was coming from below. From the deep dark where the roots of the Sun-Tree disappeared into the void.

Leo looked down. The darkness wasn't empty. It was pulsing. A rhythmic, red throb that matched the beat of the Rust Signal he had felt earlier.

"Don't look," a voice said.

Leo turned. Elder Mist was standing behind him. In the dream, the Librarian looked younger. His robes were made of flowing ink, and his eyes were solid white.

"The deep roots are not for us, Mender," Mist said, his voice echoing like it was coming from a cave. "That is the memory of the world. It is heavy."

"It's calling me," Leo said. He wasn't scared. He felt the same "tug" he felt when he saw a broken chair leg. It was the pull of something that needed to be fixed.

"It is the Rust," Mist warned. "If you go down there, you might wake up heavy."

"I'm already heavy," Leo said. He touched his chest. In the dream, the **Silver Thimble** was glowing with a fierce, white light. It was acting like a compass, pointing straight down.

Leo stepped off the bridge.

He didn't fall. He slid. The air turned into a tunnel of grey fog. The pastel dreams of the village vanished, replaced by the cold, hard geometry of iron scaffolding.

He landed on a platform of rusted metal. The air here smelled of ozone and old oil.

"Hello?" Leo called out.

The silence here was different. It wasn't the "Peaceful Silence" of the Reach. It was the "Waiting Silence" of a held breath.

A shape emerged from the fog.

It was a man. But he wasn't made of flesh. He was made of clockwork. Gears turned silently in his chest. His face was a porcelain mask that had cracked down the middle. He wore a crown of rusted spikes.

**The Iron King.** Or at least, the memory of him.

The figure raised a hand. It pointed a metal finger at Leo.

"The song..." the King rasped. His voice sounded like grinding gears. "It stopped."

Leo stepped forward. He saw the Grey Lines wrapping around the King's gears. They were thick, like chains.

"You stopped it," Leo said. "You tried to make it louder."

"No," the King whispered. "We tried... to save it. The silence... was eating us."

The King stepped closer. The heat of the Rust Signal was intense here. It felt like standing next to a blast furnace.

"Mender," the King hissed. "The Sun-Tree is dying. The silence is not a cure. It is a slow death. You must... turn the key."

The King reached into his clockwork chest and pulled out a small, golden key. It was shaped like a root.

"Turn it," the King begged. "Wake the engine. Or the lights will go out forever."

Leo reached for the key.

*WAKE UP.*

The shout didn't come from the King. It came from the sky. It was Barnaby's voice, booming like thunder.

The dream shattered.

Leo gasped, sitting bolt upright in his bed. He was drenched in sweat. His heart was hammering against his ribs. The morning sun was streaming through the window, bright and golden.

"Leo!" Barnaby was standing over him, holding a watering can like a weapon. "You were... vibrating."

Leo looked at his hands. They were shaking. He looked at his chest.

The Silver Thimble was hot. Burning hot. And wrapped around it, tangled in the leather cord, was a single, thin thread of grey wire.

It hadn't been just a dream. He had brought something back.
