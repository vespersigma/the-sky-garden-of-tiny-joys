Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 11: The Quiet Engine

Leo's workshop was usually a place of restoration. Today, it was a place of invention.

He had cleared his workbench. In the center lay three objects: the **Grey Wire** from the dream, the **Polished Acorn** from the Muffin-Mice, and a pair of spare **Azure-Goggles** that Sylas had donated (reluctantly).

"This is a terrible idea," Sylas said, pacing back and forth on the porch. "You're mixing dream-logic with Earth-logic. That's how you get explosions."

"It's not going to explode," Leo muttered, squinting through a magnifying glass. "It's going to resonate."

Barnaby sat in the corner, holding a watering can. He looked worried.

"The wire is... hungry," the golem hummed.

"It's not hungry," Leo corrected. "It's ungrounded. It's infinite potential with nowhere to go. That's why it feels heavy."

Leo picked up the acorn. He had analyzed it earlier. It wasn't just a nut; it was a biological capacitor. The Muffin-Mice had infused it with so much gratitude that it was practically vibrating.

He wrapped the Grey Wire around the acorn.

*Coil the potential. Ground the static.*

As the wire touched the nut, the grey color vanished. It turned a bright, polished silver. The wire tightened on its own, biting into the shell of the acorn.

"Okay," Leo whispered. "The coil is active. Now we need a lens."

He took the lens out of Sylas's goggles. It was made of **Sky-Quartz**. He placed it on top of the acorn.

The device looked like a tiny, primitive radio. A nut wrapped in wire with a glass eye.

"It looks like a paperweight," Sylas observed.

"It's an engine," Leo said. "The Iron Kings built engines that burned fuel to make noise. This engine burns gratitude to make... well, let's find out."

Leo placed his hand on the lens.

He didn't use the "Sanding" technique. He didn't try to smooth it out. Instead, he thought about the **Sun-Tree**. He thought about the diagram in the Forbidden Text. The tree needed "Prayer." It needed a signal.

He pushed his own "Pulse of Peace" into the device. But instead of soothing it, he *pushed*. He gave it a heartbeat.

*Thump-thump.*

The acorn glowed.

*Thump-thump.*

The wire hummed. It wasn't the screech of the Rust Signal. It was a low, steady purr. It sounded like a cat sleeping on a warm radiator.

A beam of soft, white light shot out of the lens. It hit the wall of the workshop and spread out, turning the wood transparent for a second.

"Whoa!" Sylas jumped back.

"It's working," Leo said, his voice trembling. "It's converting emotional resonance into directed energy. It's a **Harmony Drive**."

Barnaby stood up. He walked over to the device and leaned down. He closed his eyes and hummed along with it. The stone of his chest began to glow.

"It sings," Barnaby rumbled. "It sings the song of the First Mender."

Leo pulled his hand back. The light faded, but the hum remained. The engine was self-sustaining.

"We did it," Leo said, looking at Sylas. "We built a machine that doesn't scream."

Sylas grinned. "So... can we put it on a boat?"

Leo looked at the device, then at the sky where the Sun-Tree was flickering.

"We're going to have to," Leo said. "Because I think we need to take this to the Root."
