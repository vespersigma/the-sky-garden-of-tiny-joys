Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 13: The Divers of the Deep

The Mist Lakes were not made of water. They were made of memory.

Leo realized this as he tried to stand up on the surface of the lake. The "water" felt solid, like cold gelatin, but every time he moved quickly, his foot sank.

"Don't struggle," Sylas whispered, his voice trembling. "If you panic, you sink. If you sink, you forget who you are."

Sylas was sitting cross-legged on the surface of the fog, holding the broken pieces of the Quiet Engine. His goggles were cracked, and his glowing suit was flickering.

"Where are we?" Leo asked, forcing his breathing to slow down.

"The Bottom of the Basket," Sylas said. "The place where the stray threads go."

Leo looked around. The air was thick and silver. He couldn't see the sky, only a ceiling of heavy grey clouds. To his left, the massive **Rust Tower** loomed like a shadow, its iron ribs covered in black oil. It was humming, a low, painful throb that made the mist ripple.

"We need to move," Leo said. "That tower... it feels hungry."

Before they could stand, the mist in front of them began to bubble.

Three figures emerged from the lake. They didn't splash. They rose silently, like oil separating from water.

They were tall and slender, wearing tight suits made of sleek, black skin that looked like seal fur. Their faces were covered by masks made of **Sky-Quartz**, and they carried long, hooked poles.

**Mist-Walkers.**

Sylas gasped. "Don't speak," he hissed. "They don't like noise."

The lead Walker approached them. He (or she, it was impossible to tell) slid across the surface of the lake without lifting their feet. The Walker stopped in front of Leo and tilted their head.

The mask reflected Leo's face. He looked tired.

The Walker raised a hand. They made a series of rapid, fluid gestures.

*Open. Hand. Flow. Quiet.*

"I don't understand," Leo said softly.

The Walker paused. They reached into a pouch at their waist and pulled out a large, spiraled shell. It glowed with a faint blue light. An **Echo-Shell**.

The Walker held the shell to Leo's ear.

*You are loud,* a voice whispered from the shell. It wasn't the Walker's voice. It sounded like a recorded memory of a woman speaking years ago. *Your heart is beating too fast. Calm it, or the lake will swallow you.*

Leo closed his eyes. He focused on the Silver Thimble against his chest. He imagined his workshop. The smell of sawdust. The feeling of wood grain under his thumb.

*Smooth. Quiet. Still.*

The surface of the lake beneath him hardened. He felt stable.

The Walker lowered the shell. They nodded once.

*Follow,* the Walker signed.

They led Leo and Sylas away from the Rust Tower and toward a cluster of floating rocks that were tethered together by thick, glowing ropes.

As they walked, Leo looked down into the mist. Below the surface, he saw shapes. Drifting lights. Fragments of scenes. A child laughing. A boat sailing. A tree falling.

"It's memory," Leo realized. "The mist is made of things people forgot."

"That's why we dive," the Walker said.

Leo jumped. The Walker hadn't spoken with their mouth. The voice had resonated directly in Leo's head, vibrating against his skull like the hum of the Quiet Engine.

"You can speak?" Leo asked.

"We project," the Walker replied. "Words are heavy. Thoughts are light. We are the keepers of the Echoes."

They arrived at the camp. It was a collection of tents made from giant, translucent fish scales. In the center was a fire that burned with a cold, blue flame.

"Sit," the Walker projected. "Drink."

They were handed bowls of **Azure-Tea**. It was bitter and cold, but it cleared the fog from Leo's mind.

"Why are you here, Mender?" the Walker asked. "You smell of the Upper World. You smell of Sun-Wheat and hubris."

"We fell," Leo said. "We built an engine. It worked too well."

"An engine," the Walker mused. They looked at the Rust Tower in the distance. "The Iron King built an engine once. It screamed until the sky cracked."

"Mine doesn't scream," Leo said. "It purrs."

He pointed to the broken device in Sylas's lap. "Or it did. Until it broke."

The Walker leaned forward. They looked at the cracked acorn. Then they looked at Leo.

"You tried to build a Harmony Drive," the Walker projected. There was a note of surprise in their mental voice. "No one has tried that since Melodia."

"Melodia?" Leo asked. "The First Mender?"

"She was not just a Mender," the Walker said. "She was a Diver. She found the Silver Thimble in this lake."

Leo touched his chest. "Here?"

"Yes," the Walker said. "And if you want to fix your engine, you will need to find what she left behind."

The Walker pointed a long, black-gloved finger toward the Rust Tower.

"The Second Thimble," the Walker said. "It is inside the Machine."
