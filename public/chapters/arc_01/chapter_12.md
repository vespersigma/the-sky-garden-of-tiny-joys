Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 12: The Test Flight

Sylas's sky-boat, the *Drifting Leaf*, was parked in the flowerbed. It was a sleek, lightweight craft made of polished Buoyant Wood. It looked fast, even when it was standing still.

"Okay," Sylas said, holding the Quiet Engine in his hands. "Where does it go? The mast? The keel? The cup-holder?"

"Center of gravity," Leo instructed. "Mount it directly in front of the Wind-Lute. We want the vibrations to travel straight into the sails."

They strapped the engine down with strips of leather. Barnaby watched from the porch, wringing his stone hands.

"Be careful," the golem hummed. "The sky is... thin today."

"Don't worry, Big Guy," Sylas grinned, pulling his goggles down. "We're just going for a gentle cruise around the Ring. Leo, hop in."

Leo climbed onto the deck. It felt spongy under his boots. He sat down next to Sylas and gripped the railing.

"Ready?" Sylas asked. He plucked a chord on the Wind-Lute. *G-Major.*

The sails caught the Gold Current. The boat lifted gently off the grass, hovering five feet in the air. It drifted forward, smooth and silent.

"So far, so good," Leo said. "Now, turn on the engine."

Sylas reached out and tapped the **Sky-Quartz** lens.

*Thump-thump.*

The engine woke up. The low purr vibrated through the deck. The light from the lens hit the back of the main sail.

*WHOOSH.*

The boat didn't accelerate; it *leaped*.

Leo was thrown back against the cushions. The wind roared in his ears. They shot out of the garden, over the Singing Bridges, and into the open sky.

"Whoa!" Sylas laughed, his hands flying over the lute strings. "It's responding to the melody! The louder I play, the faster we go!"

"Slow down!" Leo yelled, grabbing the railing. "We're going too fast!"

"We're barely moving!" Sylas shouted back. "Watch this!"

He switched chords. *A-Minor.* He angled the boat toward a massive **Blue Current** that was rushing past them like a river of cold water.

The moment the boat hit the Blue Current, the world blurred.

The engine screamed—not with noise, but with pure harmony. It synchronized perfectly with the current. The boat became weightless. They weren't flying anymore; they were becoming part of the wind.

Leo looked down. The islands of the Middle Reach were flashing by like green blurs. They were climbing higher, faster than any sky-boat had ever gone.

"Sylas!" Leo shouted. "The lens! It's getting too hot!"

He looked at the engine. The acorn was glowing blinding white. The grey wire was vibrating so fast it was invisible.

"I can't stop it!" Sylas yelled, his grin vanishing. "The lute isn't working! The current is too strong!"

They were caught in a feedback loop. The faster they went, the more gratitude the engine generated. The more gratitude, the faster they went.

"Disconnect the wire!" Leo ordered.

He scrambled forward on his hands and knees, fighting the G-force. He reached for the engine.

The heat coming off it was intense. It felt like a sunburn. Leo gritted his teeth and grabbed the wire.

*Shock.*

It wasn't the wet cement this time. It was pure electricity. It threw him backward.

"Hold on!" Sylas screamed.

The boat banked hard. They had hit a turbulence pocket. The Blue Current shattered.

They dropped.

The sensation of weightlessness vanished, replaced by the sickening feeling of freefall. The engine sputtered and died. The sails went slack.

They plummeted through the clouds. The air turned cold and damp. The golden light of the Sun-Tree faded, replaced by a murky, twilight grey.

"Brace for impact!" Leo yelled.

They crashed.

It wasn't a hard crash. They didn't hit rock. They hit something soft and wet.

*SPLASH.*

The boat skidded across a surface that felt like cold jelly. Mist sprayed everywhere. They spun in circles, slowing down until they finally came to a stop.

Silence.

Leo opened his eyes. He was soaked. He looked around.

They were floating in a lake of silver fog. The air was thick and silent. Above them, the Middle Reach was just a distant ceiling of green.

"The Mist Lakes," Sylas whispered, his face pale. "We fell all the way to the Root Border."

Leo looked at the engine. The acorn was cracked. The wire was dull grey again.

And in the distance, through the fog, Leo saw something that made his blood run cold.

A massive, rusted iron tower was rising out of the mist. And it was humming.
