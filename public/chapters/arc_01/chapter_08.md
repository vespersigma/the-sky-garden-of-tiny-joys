Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 8: The Night of Lanterns

The sun began to set, but the world did not get dark. It simply changed colors.

The golden light of the day faded into a deep, rich indigo, and then, as the Sun-Tree took its "Inhale," the leaves turned a soft, shimmering silver. The entire sky looked like it was made of moonlight.

Leo sat on the porch of the Mending Spire, watching the transformation. He felt clean. The dip in the Stamen Well had washed away the "Sand in the Blood" feeling, leaving him light and strangely optimistic.

Barnaby was busy in the garden. The golem was carefully placing small, glowing stones around the roots of the Singing Roses. He was humming a low, vibrating tune that made the flower petals twitch in time.

"Leo!"

The voice came from the gate. It was Mina.

She was not wearing her apron. She was wearing a dress made of **Petal-Silk** that shifted color from pale pink to cream as she moved. Her hair was braided with small, glowing flowers. She looked... nice. Very nice.

Leo stood up, suddenly conscious of his own dusty work clothes. He smoothed his tunic.

"Hey, Mina," he said. "Is the bread burning?"

Mina laughed. It was a bright, clear sound. "No bread tonight, Mender. It's the Night of Lanterns. Did you forget?"

"I didn't forget," Leo lied. "I just... don't have a lantern."

"I know," Mina said, stepping onto the porch. She was holding a large, flat basket. Inside were sheets of thin paper, jars of glue that smelled like pine, and thin strips of Sky-Wood. "That's why I brought supplies. You can help me with mine."

Leo looked at the basket. "You want me to help you build a lantern?"

"It's structural engineering," Mina said, winking. "I figured you'd be into that. My frames always end up lopsided. I need a craftsman."

Leo felt a warmth in his chest that had nothing to do with magic. "Okay. I can do frames."

They sat on the porch floor. The air was cool, but the silver light from the Sun-Tree provided enough illumination to work.

Mina wanted to make a lantern shaped like a **Sun-Cake**. It was round, with pleated edges. Leo measured the wood strips with his thumb, cutting them to precise lengths with a small knife. He glued them together, his hands steady and sure.

"You have quiet hands," Mina observed, watching him work.

"Occupational hazard," Leo muttered, focusing on a corner joint. "If you have loud hands in my line of work, you break the antique."

"It's nice," she said softly.

They worked in silence for a while. It wasn't the heavy silence of the Rust Signal. It was a companionable silence. The kind of silence Leo had moved worlds to find.

When the frame was done, it was perfect. A geometric sphere of light wood. Mina covered it with the paper, smoothing it down with her flour-dusted fingers.

"Now for the wick," she said.

She pulled a single **Glow-Seed** from her pocket. It was the size of a marble and pulsed with a steady, white light.

"Hold it," she whispered.

Leo held the lantern steady. Mina placed the seed in the center cup.

"In the Reach," she said, not looking at the lantern but at Leo, "we ask someone to help light our wick when we want to share our light with them. It means... we trust them to keep the flame steady."

Leo looked at her. Her eyes were reflecting the silver light of the tree. She looked hopeful.

Leo’s brain, usually so good at analyzing friction and stress, finally clicked.

*Oh,* he thought. *This isn't a construction project. This is a date.*

He felt a flush of heat rise up his neck. He suddenly felt very clumsy.

"I... uh," Leo stammered. "The frame is sturdy. It should hold the thermal updraft efficiently."

Mina giggled. She reached out and placed her hand over his. Her hand was warm and rough from kneading dough.

"Thank you, Leo," she said. "For the structural integrity."

She stood up, holding the glowing lantern. "Come on. The launch is starting."

They walked down to the village square. It was packed with people. Every single person was holding a lantern. There were glowing sheep, glowing mice, glowing stars. The air was filled with the hum of a thousand people singing the **Sun-Song** under their breath.

*Hum low, hum slow... Let the golden rivers flow...*

Elder Thorne stood at the edge of the Stamen Well. He raised his staff.

"Release!" he called out.

Thousands of hands let go.

The lanterns drifted upward. They caught the **Green Currents** first, swirling around the village like a school of fish. Then, as they rose higher, they caught the **Gold Current**. They formed a river of fire that flowed up toward the silver branches of the Sun-Tree.

It was the most beautiful thing Leo had ever seen.

He felt Mina's shoulder brush against his. He didn't pull away. He watched the lights drift into the infinite dark, and for the first time, he didn't feel like a stranger. He felt like a knot in the weave.

"Happy Nap," Mina whispered.

"Happy Nap," Leo replied.

And high above them, unnoticed by the celebration, a single red spark flickered in the direction of the Root Isles. The Rust Signal hadn't stopped. It was just waiting for the world to go to sleep.
