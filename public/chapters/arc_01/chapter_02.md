Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 2: The Baker's Warmth

"A Mender?" Leo repeated, his voice raspy. He tried to stand, but his legs felt like they were underwater. The "Wet Cement" sensation in his veins was hardening.

The woman, Mina, didn't offer a magical glowing hand. She just reached down, grabbed his left arm with a grip that could probably crush a walnut, and hauled him to his feet.

"Careful," she said, her tone practical. "You're heavy. You sucked up a lot of static from that bird. If you don't vent it soon, you're going to pass out, and I am *not* carrying you back to the village."

Leo blinked, trying to focus. Up close, she didn't look like a ethereal elf. She looked like a baker who had been up since 4:00 AM. There was flour in her eyebrows and a smudge of something that looked like raspberry jam on her cheek. She smelled of yeast and active fermentation.

"Vent it?" Leo asked, swaying slightly. "Like... a radiator?"

"Sure. Radiator," Mina said, steering him toward a path made of crushed white stone. "Let's go with that. Come on. My bakery is just across the bridge. I have Peace-Brew on the stove. It's the only thing that dissolves the sludge."

Leo let her lead him. Not because he trusted her, but because his brain was currently too busy calculating the impossible physics of the landscape to form a coherent argument. They walked through a field of the teal grass. Leo noted that the blades didn't crush under his boots; they bent and sprang back with a rubbery resilience. *Polymer-based cellulose?* he wondered.

Then they reached the edge of the island.

Leo stopped. His craftsman's brain short-circuited.

Connecting this island to the next was a bridge. But it wasn't built. It was grown. Two massive trees on opposite cliffs had been convinced to weave their branches together into a single, seamless arch. There were no nails. No bolts. No keystones. It was a structural nightmare.

"That's not safe," Leo muttered, staring at the span. "The tension load in the center... the wood should snap."

"It's a Singing Bridge," Mina said, pulling him forward. "It doesn't hold weight with tension. It holds it with a C-Major chord. Just walk."

Leo stepped onto the wood. He expected it to creak. Instead, he felt a vibration travel up through his boots. It was a low, steady hum, like a cat purring against a floorboard. As he walked, the vibration seemed to loosen the "cement" in his legs.

"It's vibrating," Leo said, looking at his feet. "Resonant frequency damping?"

"It's shaking the dust off your soul," Mina corrected. "Look up, Mender."

Leo looked up.

Petal-Wharf wasn't a fantasy village. It was a bio-architectural complex. The "houses" were bulbous growths emerging from the trunks of massive trees, shaped like gall-knots but hollowed out and fitted with glass. It looked organic, chaotic, and completely efficient. The walkways were suspension bridges made of living vines.

"It looks like a colony of weaver birds designed a city," Leo observed.

"We like to think of it as cozy," Mina said.

She led him to a structure near the center of the cluster. It smelled intensely of caramelized sugar. The door was a slab of polished bark that slid open on a track made of smooth stone.

Inside, the heat hit Leo like a physical wall. It was a good heat—dry and smelling of bread.

"Sit," Mina commanded, pointing to a chair that looked like it had been carved from a single stump.

Leo collapsed into it. The chair was hard, but the angle was ergonomically perfect. It supported his lower back in a way his expensive office chair on Earth never had.

Mina bustled behind a counter, clinking ceramic against wood. She returned a moment later with a steaming mug. The liquid inside was a translucent, electric blue.

"Drink," she said. "Don't sip it. Drink it."

Leo looked at the blue liquid. "Is this going to make me see colors?"

"It's going to stop your heart from feeling like a brick," Mina said. "Trust the chemistry."

Leo drank.

It didn't taste like tea. It tasted like cold air. It tasted like mint and ozone. As it hit his stomach, he felt a sudden, sharp *crack* in his chest, followed by a rush of cold. The heavy "cement" sensation in his veins evaporated instantly, replaced by a light, tingling buzz.

Leo gasped, putting the cup down. The headache he hadn't realized he had was gone. The world seemed sharper. He could hear the hum of the bridge outside. He could hear the yeast bubbling in the dough on the counter.

"Whoa," he whispered. "That's... efficient."

"Peace-Brew," Mina said, wiping her hands on her apron. "Standard prescription for a Mender who doesn't know his limits."

She leaned against the counter, crossing her arms. Her eyes were sharp, evaluating him.

"Now," she said, her voice dropping the 'customer service' tone. "You have an Azure Heart-Coin in your hand, you smell like a workshop, and you look like you've never seen a Singing Bridge before. So, tell me the truth. Where exactly did you come from?"
