Arc 1: The Seed of Silence
Chapter 3: The Humming Giant

"Earth," Leo said. "I came from Earth."

Mina blinked. She didn't look shocked. She looked confused. "Earth? Is that near the Root Isles? I've heard they have a lot of dirt down there."

Leo sighed. "It's further than that. It's... never mind. Let's just say I'm not from around here."

He finished the Peace-Brew. The crack in his chest had settled into a steady, cool rhythm. He felt lighter than he had in years. It was disturbing. He was used to the weight of gravity and stress; without it, he felt like a balloon that had been untethered.

"Well," Mina said, taking his empty cup. "If you're going to stay in Petal-Wharf, you'll need somewhere to sleep. The Mending Spire has been empty since Elder Olin faded. It's on the edge of the Stem Island. A bit overgrown, but it has good bones."

"The Mending Spire," Leo repeated. The name sounded pretentious. "Does it have a roof?"

"Mostly," Mina smiled. "Go check it out. Follow the smell of rain."

Leo thanked her and stepped back out into the village. The sun was beginning to set, casting long, golden shadows across the wooden walkways. The air smelled of jasmine and cooling stone.

He followed Mina's directions, crossing two more Singing Bridges—one hummed in D-Minor, the other in G-Major—until he reached the edge of the Stem Island.

There it was.

The Mending Spire wasn't a tower. It was a massive, hollowed-out stump of white wood that spiraled upward like a drill bit. It was covered in vines that glowed with faint bioluminescence. It looked abandoned, but not dead. It looked like it was sleeping.

Leo walked through the gate. The garden was a mess. Weeds the size of cabbage heads choked the path. But as he stepped onto the grass, he froze.

He wasn't alone.

A low, rhythmic vibration was trembling through the soles of his boots. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.* It sounded like a subwoofer buried in the dirt.

Leo narrowed his eyes. "Who's there?"

No answer. Just the rustle of leaves.

He took a step forward. The vibration stopped.

"I can feel the floorboards moving," Leo said, crossing his arms. "I know something heavy is here. Come out."

To his right, a large bush of Singing Roses shuddered. The flowers, which looked like stained glass, chimed softly in protest. Slowly, remarkably slowly, a shape began to rise from behind the bush.

It was grey. It was mossy. It was enormous.

Leo craned his neck. The figure kept rising. Eight feet. Nine feet. Ten feet. It was a golem made of smooth river stones and weathered wood. Its joints were thick knots of root. Its head was a block of granite with two glowing azure crystals for eyes.

It stood there, motionless. It was trying to blend in with the tree behind it. It was doing a terrible job.

"You are not a tree," Leo noted.

The golem’s eyes dimmed slightly. A sound rumbled from its chest—a deep, sorrowful grind, like two millstones rubbing together.

"I... am attempting stealth," the golem hummed. The voice didn't come from a mouth; it resonated directly from the stone, vibrating in Leo's chest.

"You're ten feet tall," Leo said. "And you're covered in glowing moss. Stealth isn't really your strong suit."

The golem looked down at its feet. It shuffled slightly, and the sound was a soft *crump* as the soil compacted under its weight.

"I did not wish to frighten the New Mender," the golem rumbled. "I am Barnaby. I guard the snails."

Leo blinked. "Snails?"

Barnaby slowly lifted one massive hand. Cupped in his palm was a small wooden bowl. Inside, three garden snails were munching on a lettuce leaf.

"They were on the path," Barnaby explained. "You were walking heavy. I moved them to safety."

Leo looked at the giant stone war-machine holding a bowl of snails. He looked at his own hands, still stained with the memory of walnut oil. He felt the silver thimble against his chest.

For the first time since arriving, the cynicism in Leo's gut unclenched. He let out a short, dry laugh.

"Nice to meet you, Barnaby," Leo said. "I'm Leo. And I promise I won't step on your snails."

Barnaby’s eyes pulsed with a bright, happy gold light. The moss on his shoulders wiggled.

"Good," Barnaby hummed. "The Spire is dusty. I have been keeping the spiders company, but they are not good at conversation. Shall we go inside?"
