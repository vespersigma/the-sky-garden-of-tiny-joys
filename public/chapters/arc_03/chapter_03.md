Arc 3: The Sun-Tree's Sneeze
Chapter 3: The Resonance

Sylas took a deep breath. The cold air burned his lungs.

"Okay," he said, his voice shaking. "You want loud? I can do loud."

He closed his eyes. He thought about the Blue Current. He thought about the speed of the wind.

He opened his mouth and let out a note.

It wasn't a pretty note. It was a sharp, piercing shriek that sounded like a tea-kettle boiling over. It bounced off the frozen walls of the monastery, echoing back with a painful intensity.

"Hold it!" Leo shouted, pressing both hands against the ice.

He felt the vibration hit his palms. It was jagged and raw. It felt like broken glass.

*Smooth it out. Find the crack.*

Leo visualized the ice crystals. They were locked in a rigid, geometric pattern. He used Sylas's voice as a hammer. He pushed the sound into the microscopic gaps between the crystals.

*Shatter.*

A spiderweb of cracks appeared on the gate. The ice groaned.

"Louder!" Leo commanded.

Sylas took another breath and pushed harder. His face turned red. The note climbed higher, turning into a ultrasonic whine that made Leo's teeth ache.

The ice didn't melt. It exploded.

*CRASH.*

Shards of Diamond Frost rained down around them. The gate swung open.

Leo and Sylas stumbled inside, gasping for breath. The air inside the monastery was still freezing, but it was silent.

The Sun-Catchers were standing in rows, their hands raised in frozen prayer. The ice around them was thinner here, like a glaze of sugar.

"We have to wake them up gently," Leo said. "If we shock them, their hearts might stop."

He walked to the nearest monk. He placed his silver thimble against the ice.

*Warmth. Flow. Return.*

He pushed a gentle Pulse of Peace into the monk's chest. The ice hissed and evaporated into white mist.

The monk gasped, falling forward. Leo caught him.

"Easy," Leo said. "You've been asleep."

The monk looked up. His eyes were pure white, with no irises. He blinked, and his pupils slowly faded back into existence.

He looked at Leo. Then he raised his hands and made a series of rapid, fluid gestures.

*Light. Tree. Pain. Choke.*

"He's speaking Light-Hand," Sylas whispered. "I only know the basics. He says... the tree is choking."

The monk nodded. He pointed toward the central courtyard of the monastery.

They followed him.

The courtyard was an open-air platform that looked directly up at the trunk of the Sun-Tree. From here, the tree was a wall of blinding gold.

But something was wrong.

Wrapped around the trunk, about a mile above them, was a massive, purple vine. It was thick as a city block. It pulsed with a dark, rhythmic light that seemed to be sucking the gold out of the bark.

"That's not a native plant," Leo said, shielding his eyes.

The monk signed again.

*Void-Vine. Drifter. Hungry.*

"It came from the dark," Sylas translated. "It drifted in from the Unwoven Space."

"It's a parasite," Leo said. "It's drinking the Sun-Tree. That's why the tree sneezed. It's trying to shake it off."

The monk shook his head. He signed frantically.

*No. Not. Parasite.*

Sylas frowned. "He says... it's not a parasite. It's a... midwife?"

Leo looked at the vine. It wasn't attacking the tree. It was holding it. It was bracing it.

"The tree isn't sick," Leo realized, his eyes widening. "It's pregnant."

He looked at the golden dust that was still falling from the canopy.

"It's not pollen," Leo whispered. "It's a nebula. The Sun-Tree is trying to birth a new island cluster. And the vine is helping it push."
