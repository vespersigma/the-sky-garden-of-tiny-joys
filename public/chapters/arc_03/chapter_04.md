Arc 3: The Sun-Tree's Sneeze
Chapter 4: The Mending of the Birth

The scale of the Sun-Tree was impossible. From the ground, it looked like a pillar of light. From here, standing on the edge of the Monastery courtyard, it looked like a wall of living gold that stretched up forever.

"The Void-Vine is holding the branch steady," Leo said, watching the massive purple tendrils pulse with light. "But the connection point is too tight. The tree is trying to push the new islands out, but the bark won't give."

"It's a breech birth," Sylas whispered. "If it pushes too hard, the branch will snap. And if that branch snaps..."

"We lose the Sky Isles," Leo finished.

He looked at the monks. The High-Abbot was signing rapidly to his followers. They were gathering their Diamond-Nets, preparing to catch the falling debris if the worst happened.

"I need to get up there," Leo said.

"It's a mile straight up," Sylas said, pointing. "And the gravity is weird. If you slip, you won't fall down. You'll fall *out*."

"I know," Leo said. He reached into his pocket and pulled out his climbing picks. "That's why I need you to fly me."

Sylas blinked. "Fly you? My boat is back in the Middle Reach."

"Not a boat," Leo said. "We don't need a boat. We need a current."

He pointed to the air around the Sun-Tree. It was shimmering with heat. The "Sneeze" had created a massive updraft of **Gold Current**.

"If we jump," Leo said, "the current will catch us. We just need to steer."

Sylas grinned. He adjusted his goggles. "You want to body-surf a solar flare? Leo, you're finally starting to think like a local."

They ran to the edge of the platform.

"On three," Leo said. "One. Two. Three."

They jumped.

The Gold Current caught them instantly. It wasn't like falling. It was like being shot out of a cannon made of warm honey. They shot upward, past the frozen spires of the monastery, past the clouds, and into the blinding light of the upper canopy.

"Steer left!" Leo yelled over the roar of the wind.

Sylas grabbed Leo's arm and angled his body. They banked hard, skimming the surface of the Sun-Tree's bark. The wood was warm and smooth, humming with a vibration that rattled Leo's teeth.

They reached the connection point.

It was a knot the size of a mountain. A massive burl of golden wood was bulging out from the trunk. The bark was stretched thin, cracking and groaning under the pressure. Inside the burl, Leo could see swirling lights—new islands, still forming, waiting to be born.

The Void-Vine was wrapped around the burl, trying to squeeze it open, but the wood was too hard.

"It's calcified," Leo shouted. "The bark is too old. It won't stretch."

He unhooked his picks and slammed them into the wood. He hung there, suspended a mile above the world.

"What do we do?" Sylas yelled, hovering nearby.

"We don't break it," Leo said. "We soften it."

He touched his silver thimble.

*Warmth. Elasticity. Give.*

He placed his hand on the giant knot. He didn't try to sand it down. He tried to "Mass-Mend" the concept of rigidity. He visualized the wood turning into silk. He poured every ounce of his gratitude into the tree.

*Let go.*

The Friction Toll hit him like a hammer. His arm turned to ice. His vision blurred. The sheer scale of the tree was overwhelming his human soul.

"Leo!" Sylas screamed. "You're freezing!"

"I... can... do... it," Leo gasped.

He pushed harder.

*CRACK.*

It wasn't a bad crack. It was the sound of a seed pod popping open.

The burl split.

A shower of golden sap rained down. And from the opening, three small, perfectly round islands floated out. They were glowing pink and new, covered in baby Silk-Grass.

The Void-Vine gently caught them, guiding them into a stable orbit around the trunk.

The Sun-Tree let out a long, low sigh. The golden dust stopped falling. The heat wave vanished.

"It's a boy," Sylas laughed, wiping sap off his goggles. "And a girl. And... another girl?"

Leo didn't answer. He was hanging limp from his picks, his arm frozen solid. He looked at the new islands drifting away into the blue.

"They're beautiful," he whispered.

Then he passed out.
