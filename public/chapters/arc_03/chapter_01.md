Arc 3: The Sun-Tree's Sneeze
Chapter 1: The Golden Sneeze

The morning started like any other. Leo woke up, stretched, and walked to the window to greet the Sun-Tree.

Then the tree exploded.

It wasn't a violent explosion. There was no fire or shockwave. It was more like a massive, celestial sneeze.

One moment, the Sun-Tree was glowing its usual steady gold. The next, a cloud of sparkling, yellow dust erupted from the upper canopy. It rolled across the sky like a tidal wave of glitter, blotting out the blue and filling the air with the smell of intense, concentrated honey.

"That's new," Leo said, blinking.

The dust cloud hit Petal-Wharf a few seconds later.

It didn't hurt. It felt warm and tingly on the skin. But the effect on the environment was instantaneous.

*Creak. Snap. Rustle.*

Leo looked down at his garden.

The **Singing Roses**, which usually grew a few inches a week, suddenly shot up. They grew two feet in ten seconds. Their stems thickened, cracking the wooden trellis. The flowers burst open, singing a chorus so loud it sounded like a choir shouting.

"Barnaby!" Leo yelled, running out the door. "The weeds!"

Barnaby was already in the garden. He was holding a pair of shears, but he looked overwhelmed. A patch of **Silk-Grass** had wrapped around his legs and was trying to pull him down.

"The earth is... excited," Barnaby rumbled, ripping the grass away with a sound like tearing velcro. "It has too much energy."

Leo looked at his workshop. A **Dew-Vine** on the roof was growing so fast he could actually see it moving. It slithered down the chimney and poked its leafy head into the window.

"It's a Solar Flare," Leo realized. "But biological. The tree just dumped a massive load of growth hormone into the atmosphere."

Sylas dropped out of the sky. He wasn't in his boat; he was sliding down a **Gold Current** on his boots. He landed in a pile of rapidly expanding moss.

"Did you see that?" Sylas shouted, pulling a vine out of his hair. "The currents are going crazy! The Gold Current is so thick you can swim in it!"

"We see it," Leo said, grabbing a machete he had mended from a rust-blade. "Start hacking! If we don't cut this back, the Spire is going to be strangled."

For the next hour, they fought the garden. It was a battle against aggressive wholesomeness. Every time Leo cut a vine, two more grew in its place, smelling of vanilla and mocking him with beautiful flowers.

"This is ridiculous," Leo panted, wiping golden dust from his forehead. "I'm being attacked by a salad."

Suddenly, a bright flash of light cut through the pollen fog.

*Flash. Flash. Flash.*

It was coming from the highest point in the sky—the **High Reach**.

"A signal," Leo said, shielding his eyes. "From the Sun-Catchers."

"They never signal," Sylas said, looking worried. "They just sit up there and meditate."

"Three flashes," Leo counted. "That's not a greeting. That's an SOS."

He looked at the Sun-Tree. The golden dust was still falling, covering the world in a layer of hyper-active life.

"The tree isn't just sneezing," Leo said. "It's sick."
