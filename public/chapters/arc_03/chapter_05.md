Arc 3: The Sun-Tree's Sneeze
Chapter 5: The Recovery

Leo woke up to the smell of absolute purity.

It wasn't the smell of flowers or bread. It was the smell of light. It smelled like warm laundry and cold water at the same time.

He opened his eyes. He was lying on a bed of transparent crystal. The ceiling above him was made of glass, and beyond it, the stars were burning brighter than he had ever seen them.

"You slept for three days," a voice said.

It wasn't Sylas. The voice was in his head. It was a projection.

Leo sat up. His right arm was wrapped in bandages made of **Diamond-Thread**. It throbbed slightly, but the freezing cold was gone.

Standing at the foot of his bed was the **High-Abbot** of the Sun-Catchers. He was tall, thin, and his skin glowed with a faint pearlescent light.

"You saved the branch," the Abbot projected. "The tree is resting. The new islands have stabilized."

Leo looked at his arm. "I just sanded the bark. It wasn't magic."

"To the tree, care is magic," the Abbot replied.

The Abbot stepped forward. He held out a small, glass jar. Inside, floating in a suspension of golden liquid, was a single seed. It wasn't a normal Sun-Seed. It was blindingly bright. It looked like a miniature star.

"A **Prime-Seed**," the Abbot said. "It fell from the burl when it opened. It belongs to the Mender."

Leo took the jar. It was warm. It felt heavy, like it contained the gravity of a planet.

"What does it do?" Leo asked.

"It grows," the Abbot said simply. "Plant it when you are ready to build something permanent."

***

The trip back to Petal-Wharf was fast. The Sun-Catchers lent them a gravity-sled that slid down the **Gold Current** like a bobsled. Sylas drove, whooping with joy the entire way down.

When they broke through the cloud layer and saw the village, Leo groaned.

"Oh no."

From the air, Petal-Wharf looked less like a village and more like a salad bowl. The **Growth Pulse** hadn't just affected the Sun-Tree; it had supercharged everything in the Middle Reach.

The bridges were covered in moss so thick it looked like fur. The houses were sprouting new balconies. And Leo's workshop... well, it was gone.

It was buried under a mountain of **Singing Roses**.

"My roof," Leo whispered.

They landed on the dock. It was covered in ferns.

Mina was waiting for them. She was holding a machete in one hand and a basket of Sun-Cakes in the other.

"You're alive!" she yelled, running over to hug him. She smelled of pine sap instead of flour. "And you're late. The weeds are winning."

"I was busy birthing a solar system," Leo defended himself.

"Well, your garden didn't get the memo," Mina said, pointing a thumb at the Spire.

Leo looked. In the middle of the rose-jungle, he saw a large, grey shape suspended ten feet in the air.

It was Barnaby.

The golem had been lifted off the ground by a particularly aggressive vine. He was dangling helplessly, his stone feet kicking the air.

"This is... undignified," Barnaby hummed, seeing Leo. "The flowers are very strong."

Leo laughed. He felt the weight of the Prime-Seed in his pocket. He felt the warmth of Mina's hand on his arm. He looked at his ridiculous, overgrown, magical home.

He pulled out his machete.

"Okay," Leo said. "Let's do some gardening."

**End of Arc 3**
