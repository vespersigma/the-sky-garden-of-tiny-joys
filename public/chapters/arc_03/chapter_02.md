Arc 3: The Sun-Tree's Sneeze
Chapter 2: The Diamond Climb

"I cannot go," Barnaby said.

He was standing at the base of the Great Spiral Bridge that connected the Middle Reach to the Sky Isles. It wasn't a wooden bridge. It was a chain of floating rocks, each one smaller than the last, leading up into the blinding white light of the upper atmosphere.

"Why not?" Leo asked, adjusting his **Current-Cloak**. "We can rope you together. We can use the sky-boat."

"The air," Barnaby hummed, pointing up. "It is too thin. My moss will freeze. And without the moss, my joints will lock. I will become a statue."

Leo looked at the golem. Barnaby was covered in vibrant green moss, which was currently blooming with tiny yellow flowers thanks to the pollen storm.

"Okay," Leo said. "You stay here. Guard the Spire. Don't let the vines eat the kitchen."

"I will prune with extreme prejudice," Barnaby promised.

Leo turned to Sylas. "Ready?"

Sylas was shivering. He was wearing three layers of **Cloud-Wool**, but his teeth were still chattering.

"I hate the High Reach," Sylas muttered. "It's unnatural. The sun is right there, but it's freezing."

"It's thermodynamic efficiency," Leo said, checking his pack. He had brought a thermos of **Sun-Drop Infusion**, his silver thimble, and a pair of climbing picks he had mended from old garden trowels. "The air is too thin to hold the heat. Let's move."

They began the climb.

The transition was abrupt. One moment, they were surrounded by warm, honey-smelling air. The next, they stepped through a layer of mist and everything turned sharp.

The air here was crystal clear. The sky wasn't blue; it was a deep, terrifying indigo. The islands weren't lush green hills; they were jagged spires of white granite that looked like teeth.

"Cold," Sylas gasped, his breath turning into instant ice crystals.

"Drink," Leo ordered, handing him the thermos.

Sylas took a sip of the Sun-Drop Infusion. Color returned to his cheeks. "Better."

They climbed for hours. The gravity here was weak. Leo felt like he weighed as much as a feather. He had to be careful not to jump too hard, or he would float off into the void.

Finally, they reached the peak of the **Diamond Spire**.

Nestled between two massive shards of white stone was the **Sun-Catcher Monastery**. It was a building made entirely of glass and light. It should have been beautiful.

It wasn't. It was frozen.

A thick layer of **Diamond Frost** covered the building. It wasn't soft snow. It was hard, jagged crystal. It looked like the building had been encased in rock candy.

"That's not normal," Sylas whispered. "Diamond Frost is supposed to float. It's not supposed to stick."

Leo walked up to the main gate. It was frozen shut. He touched the ice. It burned his fingers with cold.

"It's not just frost," Leo said. "It's leakage. The Sun-Seeds inside the monastery... they've ruptured. The raw light is leaking out and freezing on contact with the air."

He looked through the transparent wall. Inside, he saw figures standing perfectly still. The Sun-Catchers. They were frozen in mid-step, encased in blocks of amber ice.

"They're statues," Sylas said, horrified.

"No," Leo said, spotting a faint pulse of light in the chest of the nearest monk. "They're in stasis. They're preserving their energy. We have to get them out."

Leo raised his hand. He visualized the "Hum" of the ice. It was a high, screeching frequency.

"I'm going to need you to sing, Sylas," Leo said. "High pitch. Match the tone of the frost."

"I don't know the song," Sylas said.

"Then improvise," Leo said, placing his palm against the frozen gate. "Just make it loud."
