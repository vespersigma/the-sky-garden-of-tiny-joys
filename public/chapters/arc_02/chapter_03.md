Arc 2: The Current War
Chapter 3: The Missing Song

"They're lying," Sylas said, pacing the floor of Leo's workshop. "They have to be lying. The Quiet Engine uses **Gratitude**. It creates energy; it doesn't consume it. That's the whole point."

Leo sat at his workbench, staring at the disassembled engine. The acorn was glowing softly, humming its happy little tune. It looked innocent. It looked like hope.

"What if they're right?" Leo asked quietly. "We tested it in the Blue Current. We saw how fast it went. It pulled so much power that we broke the sound barrier without trying. Where did that power come from?"

"From the wind," Sylas argued. "From the resonance."

"Maybe," Leo said. "Or maybe we were drinking from a straw that was connected to someone else's cup."

Barnaby was standing by the window, looking at the black ice wall that now encircled the village.

"The whales are silent," the golem hummed. "I have not heard the Deep Song since the engine woke up."

That was enough for Leo. Barnaby's senses were tied to the earth itself. If he said the song was gone, it was gone.

"We have to go check," Leo said, standing up. "We have to go to the Outer Ring."

"How?" Sylas asked. "The Blue Current is frozen. If we try to fly through the blockade, the Keepers will shoot us down."

"We don't fly through it," Leo said. "We fly under it."

***

They took the *Drifting Leaf*. They didn't mount the engine. They used the old sails.

Sylas piloted them down, skimming the tops of the trees in the **Green Current**. It was slow, agonizing travel. The air was thick with the smell of pine and damp earth. They slipped beneath the floating blockade, hugging the underside of the Middle Reach islands until they reached the open air of the **Shadow Border**.

The moment they crossed the line, the temperature dropped.

The light of the Moon-Tree was faint here, a pale grey smear in the distance. The islands of the Outer Ring were jagged rocks covered in purple **Chemo-Moss**.

"It's quiet," Sylas whispered. "Too quiet."

Leo leaned over the railing. He scanned the horizon with a pair of binoculars he had mended from a broken telescope.

"There," he said, pointing.

Drifting near a large asteroid was a shape that looked like a storm cloud. It was massive, easily the size of three village islands combined. It was a **Sky-Whale**.

But it wasn't swimming. It was listing to one side. Its skin, usually a vibrant, glowing blue, was dull and grey. The barnacles on its back, which should have been shining like stars, were dark.

"It's beached," Sylas breathed. "It's grounded on the mana-field."

They brought the boat closer. The whale let out a low, wheezing sound. It wasn't a song. It was a gasp.

Leo felt a sharp pain in his chest. The Friction Toll. But he hadn't mended anything. He was feeling the whale's pain just by being near it.

"Get me closer," Leo ordered.

Sylas brought the boat alongside the whale's flank. The creature's skin was rough, like cold sandpaper.

Leo reached out. He placed his hand on the whale's side.

He didn't use the thimble. He needed to feel this raw.

*Empty.*

That was the only word. The whale wasn't sick. It wasn't injured. It was empty. Its internal mana reserves were completely drained. It was like a battery that had been sucked dry.

Leo closed his eyes and followed the "flow." He visualized where the whale's energy had gone. He saw a thread of blue light leaving the whale, traveling thousands of miles back toward the Middle Reach, and ending...

Leo gasped and pulled his hand back.

The thread ended at his workshop.

"It's true," Leo whispered, his face pale. "The engine. When we hit the Blue Current, we didn't just ride the wind. We pulled the 'Current-Pressure' from the entire system to fuel our acceleration. We stalled the flow."

He looked at Sylas. "We starved them."

Sylas looked at the engine parts wrapped in canvas on the deck. He looked sick.

"We have to fix it," Sylas said. "We have to turn it off."

"It is off," Leo said. "But the damage is done. The flow is broken. The whales need a jump-start, or they will fall into the void."

Barnaby rumbled, pointing at the distance. "Ships."

Three black ships were emerging from the shadow of a nearby asteroid. The Silence-Keepers had found them.

"They aren't here to arrest us," Leo realized, seeing the weapons mounted on the decks. "They're here to make sure we don't finish the job."
