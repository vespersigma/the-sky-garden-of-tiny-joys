Arc 2: The Current War
Chapter 4: The Jump-Start

The black ships were closing in. They moved silently, like sharks in deep water.

"They're charging their dampeners," Sylas yelled, his hands flying over the controls of the *Drifting Leaf*. "If they hit us with a Silence Beam, the wind will stop. We'll drop like a stone."

Leo wasn't looking at the ships. He was looking at the whale.

The massive creature let out another wheezing gasp. Its eye, which was the size of a dinner table, rolled back. It was fading.

"I need the engine," Leo said.

"It's broken!" Sylas shouted. "The capacitor is cracked!"

"I don't need the capacitor," Leo said, his voice strangely calm. "I need the coil."

He grabbed the grey wire. It was cold and dead. He wrapped it around his own arm, tightening it until it bit into his skin.

"Leo, what are you doing?" Barnaby rumbled. The golem had stepped between Leo and the approaching ships, shielding him with his stone body.

"The engine pulls gratitude from the air and turns it into thrust," Leo said. "But the air is empty. So I have to be the battery."

He placed his other hand on the whale's flank.

"Sylas," Leo commanded. "Play the song. The loudest, most joyful song you know."

"But the dampeners—"

"Just play!"

Sylas grabbed his Wind-Lute. He didn't play a delicate melody. He strummed a hard, fast chord. *C-Major. Allegro.*

The wire on Leo's arm flared to life.

*Thump.*

Leo felt the kickback instantly. It wasn't the wet cement of entropy. It was fire. He was channeling the raw mana of the lute through his own nervous system and pushing it into the whale.

"Wake up," Leo gritted out.

He visualized the whale's heart. It was a massive, slow-beating drum. He didn't try to mend it; he tried to **Shock** it. He pushed every happy memory he had—the smell of Mina's bread, the sound of Barnaby's hum, the warmth of the Sun-Tree—into the wire.

The whale shuddered.

*Thump-THUMP.*

Behind them, the lead Silence-Keeper ship fired. A beam of grey light shot toward the boat.

Barnaby roared. He caught the beam with his chest. The stone cracked, but he didn't move.

"Keep playing!" Leo screamed.

Sylas shredded the strings. The music was frantic, desperate, and beautiful.

Leo felt his knees buckle. The drain was too much. His vision was tunneling. He was pouring his own life into the beast.

*Just a little more.*

The whale's eye opened. It glowed with a blinding azure light. The barnacles on its back ignited like a constellation.

The whale opened its mouth and **Sang**.

It wasn't a note. It was a shockwave. A pulse of pure, resonant bass that shook the very foundations of the sky.

*BOOOOOOOOOOOM.*

The sound hit the Silence-Keeper ships. Their black sails shattered. The grey dampening fields dissolved instantly.

The shockwave traveled upward. It hit the frozen Blue Current. The ice cracked. With a sound like a thunderclap, the blockade shattered. The current began to flow again, rushing through the sky like a river breaking a dam.

Leo fell back onto the deck. His arm was smoking.

The whale turned. It looked at the tiny boat. It raised a massive fin in a gesture that looked almost like a salute. Then, with a powerful sweep of its tail, it dove into the deep mist, singing a song of freedom.

The Silence-Keepers retreated. They couldn't fight the song. Their silence had been broken.

"We did it," Sylas breathed, lowering his lute.

Barnaby knelt beside Leo. He touched Leo's scorched arm with a gentle stone finger.

"You are... very loud," the golem hummed approvingly.

Leo smiled weakly. "Let's go home."
