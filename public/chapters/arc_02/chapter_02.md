Arc 2: The Current War
Chapter 2: The Cold Tea

The Blue Current wasn't just frozen. It was dead.

Leo stared at the wall of ice suspended in the sky. It wasn't clear ice; it was black ice, opaque and heavy. It blocked the sunlight from reaching the northern side of Petal-Wharf, casting a long, cold shadow over the village.

"They can't just block a current," Sylas hissed, his hands twitching toward his goggles. "That's like blocking an artery. The pressure will build up until the whole Ring explodes."

"They know that," Elder Thorne said quietly. "They are counting on it."

The Silence-Keepers had set up a perimeter around their black ships. They stood like statues, their bone masks reflecting the pale light of the Moon-Tree. They didn't speak to the villagers. They didn't even look at them.

"We need to talk to them," Leo said. "We need to understand their logic. They aren't the Iron Kings. They aren't trying to destroy the tree. They think they are saving it."

"They are fanatics," Thorne warned. "They listen only to the void."

"Everyone listens to something," Leo said. "I'm going to make tea."

***

Leo set up a small table in the middle of the dock, exactly halfway between the village and the black ships. He laid out a white silk cloth. He placed two ceramic cups and a clay teapot.

He chose his leaves carefully. He didn't use **Peace-Brew** (too medicinal) or **Sun-Drop** (too aggressive). He chose **Truth-Root**. It was a tea that tasted like earth and rain. It was neutral. It was honest.

He lit a small **Sun-Seed** under the pot. The water began to boil with a soft *glub-glub* sound.

Leo sat down and waited.

He waited for an hour. The Keepers didn't move.

He waited for two hours. The sun began to set. The air grew colder.

Finally, the leader of the Keepers walked forward. He moved with a stiff, unnatural grace. He stopped at the edge of the table.

"You are performing a ritual," the Keeper stated. His voice was muffled by the bone mask.

"I'm making tea," Leo said. "It's cold out. Sit."

The Keeper didn't sit. He looked at the teapot.

"You believe that shared consumption creates a bond," the Keeper said. "You believe that if we drink together, we become the same."

"It's a start," Leo said. He poured the tea. The liquid was clear, steaming slightly. "This is Truth-Root. If we drink it, we have to speak plainly. No riddles. No threats. Just the reason why you are hurting my home."

The Keeper looked at the cup. For a second, Leo thought he saw a hesitation. A twitch of a finger.

"We do not hurt," the Keeper said. "We silence."

"Why?" Leo asked. "The world is singing again. The Moon-Tree is blooming. Why do you want it to stop?"

"Because the song is a lie," the Keeper said.

He reached out. He took the cup.

Leo's heart lifted. *He's accepting the ritual.*

The Keeper held the cup for a moment. He looked at the steam rising from the liquid.

Then, slowly and deliberately, he turned his hand over.

The tea splashed onto the white silk cloth. It stained the fabric a dark, ugly brown. It was an insult so profound that the villagers watching from the shore gasped in unison.

In Verdant Reach, spilling tea was not an accident. It was a declaration that the other person did not exist. It was a statement that their soul was not worth acknowledging.

"Your truth is loud," the Keeper said, dropping the empty cup onto the wet cloth. "It drowns out the warning."

"What warning?" Leo asked, his voice hard.

The Keeper pointed at the sky. Not at the Sun-Tree, but past it. Toward the **Outer Ring**.

"The Whales," the Keeper said. "They have stopped singing."

Leo looked up. He strained his ears. He listened for the low, vibrating hum of the Sky-Whales that usually underpinned reality.

He heard nothing.

The deep sky was silent.

"Your engine," the Keeper said, stepping back. "Your 'Harmony Drive.' It pulled the mana from the currents so fast that it starved the outer streams. You fixed your boat, Mender. But you stranded the guardians."

The Keeper turned and walked back to his ship.

"Destroy the engine," he called back. "Or we will freeze the rest of the sky."
