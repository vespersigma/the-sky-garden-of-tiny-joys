Arc 2: The Current War
Chapter 1: The Visitors

The peace lasted for exactly thirty days.

During "The Nap," Petal-Wharf was a paradise. The new Moon-Tree glowed silver every night, its light cool and restful. The villagers slept deeper than they ever had. Even Barnaby seemed to move with less weight, his stone joints greased by the ambient harmony.

Leo spent his days in the workshop, refining the **Quiet Engine**. He had added a series of **Sky-Quartz** lenses to focus the beam, turning it from a simple thruster into a precision tool.

"It's not just an engine," Leo explained to Sylas, who was currently trying to juggle three Muffin-Mice. "It's a tuning fork. If I aim it at a Frayed object, I can mend it from a distance."

"Boring," Sylas yawned, dropping a mouse. It squeaked indignantly and scampered away. "When do we fly it again?"

"When I'm sure it won't explode," Leo said.

He picked up his tea. It was a cup of **Sun-Drop Infusion**, warm and golden. He took a sip, savoring the taste of liquid sunlight.

Then, the tea rippled.

It wasn't a big ripple. It was a tiny, concentric circle that started in the center of the cup and hit the edges with a sharp *clink*.

Leo frowned. He put the cup down.

*Thrum.*

The floorboards vibrated. It felt like the Rust Signal, but sharper. More focused.

"Barnaby," Leo called out.

The golem was in the garden, pruning the Singing Roses. He stood up, his head tilting toward the sky.

"The air is... sharp," Barnaby rumbled.

Leo walked to the porch. He looked up at the **Blue Current** that flowed past the island.

Usually, the current was a smooth river of azure light. Today, it was choppy. White-capped waves of mana were breaking against the invisible banks. And riding those waves, cutting through the turbulence like knives, were three ships.

They weren't like the friendly, rounded sky-boats of the Reach. These ships were long, narrow, and made of black **Star-Metal**. Their sails were dark grey, and they didn't have Wind-Lutes. They moved in perfect, silent unison.

"Visitors," Sylas said, stepping onto the porch. He pulled his goggles down. "And they aren't from the neighborhood."

The ships descended. They didn't glide; they dropped. They landed on the main dock of Petal-Wharf with a heavy, metallic *thud* that shook the entire village.

Leo grabbed his cloak. "Stay here, Barnaby. You're too big a target."

"I will watch," Barnaby said, his eyes dimming to a protective blue.

Leo and Sylas ran down to the docks. A crowd had already gathered. Elder Thorne stood at the front, his wooden staff glowing with a faint green light.

"Welcome," Thorne called out, his voice steady. "The Stamen Well is open to all."

A ramp extended from the lead ship. It hit the wood of the dock with a harsh scrape.

A group of figures marched down. They were tall and thin, wearing robes of grey silk that seemed to absorb the light. Their faces were covered by masks made of white bone.

**Silence-Keepers.**

The leader stopped in front of Thorne. He didn't bow. He didn't offer a tea ritual. He simply pointed a gloved hand at the Mending Spire in the distance.

"You have broken the Silence," the Keeper said. His voice was flat, devoid of any melody.

"We planted a tree," Thorne replied. "We restored the balance."

"You built a machine," the Keeper corrected. "We heard the hum. It is artificial. It is a perversion of the Weaver's Dream."

Leo stepped forward. He felt the weight of the Silver Thimble against his chest.

"The machine runs on gratitude," Leo said. "It's harmless."

The Keeper turned his bone mask toward Leo.

"Gratitude is messy," the Keeper said. "Chaos is messy. We are here to restore Order. Hand over the engine, Mender. Or we will close the currents."

Leo looked at the ships. He looked at the villagers, who were huddled together, scared. He looked at the Moon-Tree, glowing softly in the daylight.

"No," Leo said.

The Keeper didn't shout. He didn't argue. He simply raised his hand.

Behind him, the sails of the black ships turned from grey to a deep, void-black. The air around the docks suddenly grew cold. The Blue Current above them stopped flowing. It froze, turning into a solid wall of ice.

"Then we will wait," the Keeper said. "Until you are quiet again."
