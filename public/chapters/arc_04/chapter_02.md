Arc 4: The Festival of Colors
Chapter 2: The Ingredient Hunt

"Moon-Vanilla," Mina said, staring at her recipe book. "It's the only way."

Leo, Mina, and Barnaby were huddled around the kitchen table in the bakery. The air smelled of stress-baking (burnt almonds).

"Master Crumb is using **Synthetic Sweetener**," Mina explained. "It's chemically perfect. It hits the exact dopamine receptors in the brain. If I use regular sugar, I lose."

"So we need magic sugar," Leo said.

"Moon-Vanilla," Mina corrected. "It grows in the High Reach, in the shadow of the Sun-Catcher Monastery. It has a flavor profile that tastes like... well, it tastes like being loved."

"I like being loved," Barnaby hummed. "I will carry the basket."

"It's a protected species," Leo warned. "The Sun-Catchers guard it. We can't just take it."

"We won't take it," Mina said, tying her apron tight. "We'll ask for it."

***

The trip up to the High Reach was much easier now. The new islands Leo had birthed in Arc 3 formed a convenient staircase. They didn't need to body-surf a solar flare; they just hiked.

When they reached the Monastery, the ice was gone. The building was glowing with a soft, warm light. The Sun-Catchers were busy polishing the glass walls.

The High-Abbot met them at the gate. He smiled when he saw Leo.

*Mender,* he signed. *Welcome back.*

"Hello, Abbot," Leo said. "This is Mina. She's the one who feeds me."

The Abbot bowed deeply to Mina. *The Sustainer. We are honored.*

"We need a favor," Mina said, her voice shaking slightly. She held out a small basket of her best muffins. "I need Moon-Vanilla. Just one pod. I'm... I'm trying to save the soul of baking."

The Abbot looked at the muffins. He took one and took a small bite. His eyes widened. He signed rapidly.

*Warmth. Memory. Yes.*

He led them to a secluded garden behind the monastery. It was a zero-gravity zone. Floating in the air were delicate, white orchids. They didn't have stems; they just drifted like jellyfish.

"Moon-Vanilla," Mina whispered.

She floated up to the nearest flower. It was closed tight.

"It won't open for force," the Abbot signed. "It only opens for a song."

Mina looked at Leo. Leo smiled.

"You're the Flour-Singer," Leo said. "Sing to it."

Mina closed her eyes. She didn't sing a complex opera. She hummed a simple, quiet lullaby. It was the song she hummed when she was kneading dough at 4:00 AM. It was a song about patience, heat, and the promise of a rising loaf.

The orchid shivered. Slowly, its petals unfolded. A rich, dark scent filled the air. It smelled like cream, old books, and starlight.

A single, long vanilla bean dropped into Mina's hand.

"Thank you," she whispered to the flower.

The Abbot clapped silently. Barnaby wiped a tear of moss from his eye.

"We have the flavor," Leo said. "Now let's go beat a machine."
