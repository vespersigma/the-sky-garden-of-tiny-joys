Arc 4: The Festival of Colors
Chapter 4: The Tasting

The baking was done. The air in the village square was thick with the scent of caramelized sugar and warm flour.

On the judges' table sat two cakes.

Master Crumb's creation was a geometric marvel. It was a perfect sphere of gold sponge, glazed with a mirror-finish of spun sugar. It floated three inches above the plate, held aloft by a tiny anti-gravity charm.

"It is flawless," Crumb announced, wiping a speck of dust from his monocle. "I have calculated the crumb density to the nanometer. It is the Platonic Ideal of a cake."

Mina's cake was... rustic. It was a simple, round loaf, slightly lopsided on the left. The crust was a deep, rich brown, and you could see the flecks of Moon-Vanilla in the glaze. It didn't float. It sat heavily on the plate, looking warm and solid.

"It looks like a rock," Crumb sneered.

"It looks like breakfast," Leo said, standing beside Mina.

Elder Bloom stepped forward. She rang a small silver bell.

"Bring out the Judges!"

The crowd went silent. From the roof of the bakery, a tiny rope ladder was lowered. Down climbed three **Muffin-Mice**.

They were the Elders of the Mouse Council. Their fur was grey with age, and they wore tiny sashes made of blue silk. They scurried onto the table, their noses twitching.

They approached Crumb's cake first.

The lead mouse sniffed the floating sphere. It reached out a paw and touched the glaze. It was cold. The mouse shivered. It took a tiny bite.

The mouse chewed slowly. It swallowed. Then it looked at Crumb, let out a polite but dismissive *squeak*, and walked away.

"What?" Crumb gasped. "That is impossible! The sugar content is optimal!"

"It has no soul," Leo whispered. "It's just math."

The mice moved to Mina's cake.

As they got closer, their whiskers started to vibrate. The heat coming off the cake was visible. It smelled of the Moon-Vanilla, but it also smelled of the song Mina had hummed while kneading it.

The lead mouse didn't just take a bite. It buried its face in the soft crumb. It closed its eyes and let out a long, happy *purr* that was amplified by the microphone on the table.

The crowd erupted.

"They're purring!" Sylas shouted. "It's a Ten-Squeak score!"

The other two mice joined in. They ate with abandon, ignoring the crumbs on their whiskers. When they were full, they didn't leave. They curled up on top of the remaining cake and fell asleep.

Elder Bloom smiled. "I believe we have a verdict. The mice have chosen their bed."

Master Crumb stared at his perfect, untouched sphere. He looked at the sleeping mice. He looked at Mina, who was crying happy tears into her apron.

For the first time, the haughty baker looked small.

"I measured everything," Crumb whispered to himself. "I measured the heat. I measured the flour. I measured the time."

Leo stepped closer to him.

"You forgot to measure the friction," Leo said gently. "You tried to force the dough to rise. Mina asked it nicely."

Crumb looked at Leo. He adjusted his monocle, but his hand was shaking.

"Can you..." Crumb hesitated. "Can you teach me how to ask?"
