Arc 4: The Festival of Colors
Chapter 1: The Golden Apron

It took three days to clear the vines off the Mending Spire.

Leo and Barnaby worked in shifts. Barnaby pulled the heavy roots, humming a low, steady bass note that encouraged the plants to let go. Leo used his machete, sanding the cut ends so they wouldn't grow back.

By the time they were done, the village of Petal-Wharf was unrecognizable. It was greener, lush, and smelled intensely of jasmine. The houses looked like they were wearing floral hats.

"It is... cozy," Barnaby decided, wiping sap from his stone cheek.

"It's a fire hazard," Leo muttered, but he was smiling.

They walked down to the village square. The mood in Petal-Wharf was electric. The **Day of First Bloom** was approaching, which meant the start of the **Grand Bake-Off**.

A stage had been set up near the Stamen Well. Elder Bloom stood in the center, holding a shimmering piece of fabric.

"Behold!" she announced. "The Golden Apron!"

The crowd cheered. The apron wasn't just gold-colored; it was woven from **Sun-Silk**. It glowed with its own internal light. Whoever wore it would be recognized as the Master Baker of the Middle Reach for a full year.

Mina stood next to Leo. She was wringing her hands in her own flour-dusted apron.

"It's beautiful," she whispered. "But I don't stand a chance."

"Why not?" Leo asked. "Your Sun-Cakes are literally magical. I ate one yesterday and I stopped worrying about my taxes for an hour. And I don't even pay taxes here."

"Because of him," Mina said, pointing.

A sky-boat was descending toward the dock. It wasn't a humble wooden vessel like the *Drifting Leaf*. It was a sleek, polished catamaran made of white enamel and brass. It didn't have sails; it had **Rotors** powered by concentrated Sun-Seeds.

It landed with a precise, mechanical *thud*.

A ramp extended. A man walked down. He was tall, thin, and wore a chef's uniform that was so white it hurt to look at. He carried a silver cane and wore a monocle that looked suspiciously like Master Archi's.

"Master Crumb," Mina hissed. "The High Baker of the Inner Ring."

Crumb didn't walk; he strutted. He was followed by three assistants carrying large, metal crates.

"People of the Reach!" Crumb announced, his voice projected by a small acoustic amplifier on his collar. "I have arrived. You may now begin... applauding."

The crowd clapped politely.

Crumb walked up to Elder Bloom. He bowed, but it was a shallow, technical bow.

"I see the village is... quaint," Crumb said, sniffing the air. "It smells of uncontrolled yeast and inconsistent oven temperatures."

He snapped his fingers. His assistants opened the crates.

Inside was a machine. It looked like a cross between a stove and a laboratory. It had glass tubes, brass dials, and a chamber filled with glowing blue fire.

"The **Gravity-Whisk 3000**," Crumb declared. "It kneads dough at 5,000 rotations per minute. It bakes with focused light-beams. It produces a cake with zero percent variance."

The crowd murmured. It was impressive. It was loud.

Mina shrank back. "See? I use a wooden spoon. He uses physics."

Leo looked at Crumb's machine. He saw the Grey Lines of stress vibrating in the brass gears. It was efficient, yes. But it was cold.

"Physics has no flavor," Leo whispered to Mina. He touched her shoulder. "Your spoon has a soul. His machine just has a manual."

Mina looked at him. She smiled, just a little.

"You're biased," she said.

"I'm a Mender," Leo replied. "I know the difference between a tool and a friend."
