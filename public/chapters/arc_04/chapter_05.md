Arc 4: The Festival of Colors
Chapter 5: The Golden Apron

The sun was setting by the time the cheering stopped.

Mina stood on the stage, the **Golden Apron** tied around her waist. It shimmered with a soft, internal light that matched the glow of the Sun-Tree. She looked tired, messy, and happier than Leo had ever seen her.

"I won," she whispered, looking at the crowd.

"You won," Leo confirmed, handing her a fresh cup of tea.

Master Crumb walked over to them. He had cleaned the oil off his face, but his white uniform was still stained. He looked humbled.

"I have tasted the bread," Crumb said, bowing deeply to Mina. "It is superior. My machine... it lacked the conversation."

Mina smiled. She broke a piece of her prize-winning Sun-Cake and offered it to him.

"Here," she said. "It's still warm."

Crumb took it. He ate it slowly, closing his eyes. A single tear leaked out from under his monocle.

"It tastes like my grandmother's kitchen," he whispered.

He turned to Leo.

"And you, Mender," Crumb said. "You saved my life's work. You fixed the machine that I used to try and defeat you."

Crumb reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, heavy coin. It wasn't Azure. It wasn't Amber. It was **Gold**.

"A Gold Heart-Coin," Elder Bloom gasped from the side of the stage. "Those only manifest for Acts of Forgiveness."

Crumb placed the coin in Leo's hand. It was hot. It vibrated with a deep, resonant hum that felt like a cello string.

"Thank you," Crumb said. "For teaching me that efficiency is not the same as quality."

***

The feast lasted until midnight.

The village square was filled with tables groaning under the weight of Sun-Cakes, Cloud-Cream, and roasted root-vegetables. Barnaby sat at the head of the table, wearing a flower crown that Sylas had woven for him. He was happily eating a salad the size of a hay bale.

Leo sat on the edge of the Stamen Well, watching the party.

He felt the weight of the Gold Coin in his pocket. He felt the cool metal of the Silver Thimbles around his neck. He looked at Mina, who was laughing with Elder Aria. He looked at Sylas, who was trying to teach a Muffin-Mouse how to juggle.

He realized something.

On Earth, he had always been waiting for the weekend. He had been waiting for the noise to stop. He had been waiting for his life to start.

Here, in the glow of the Moon-Tree, he wasn't waiting for anything.

"Hey," Mina said, sitting down next to him. She handed him a plate of cake. "You look serious."

"Just thinking," Leo said.

"About what?"

"About friction," Leo said. "I used to think my job was to remove it. To sand everything down until it was smooth and perfect."

He looked at the chaotic, loud, messy party. He looked at the cracked stone of Barnaby's chest. He looked at the flour in Mina's hair.

"But maybe," Leo said, taking a bite of the cake, "some friction is necessary. It's how we know we're touching something real."

Mina leaned her head on his shoulder.

"Welcome home, Leo," she whispered.

Leo smiled. He closed his eyes and listened to the hum of the world. It wasn't silent. It was singing.

**End of Arc 4**
